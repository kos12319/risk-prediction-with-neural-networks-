**Interpretation:**
- TreeSHAP confirms that the same features driving split-based variable importance also contribute the most to prediction adjustments on held-out data. The similar ordering means the tree ensemble’s decisions genuinely pivot on these columns rather than exploiting noise.
- Term structure (`36/60 months`) and FICO-level features dominate, but **credit depth and utilization** (`mort_acc`, `num_actv_rev_tl`, `total_rev_hi_lim`, `total_bc_limit`) have non-trivial contributions, explaining why H2O outperforms the sparse L1 subset.
- Engineered ratios (`income_to_loan_ratio`, `fico_avg`) carry >5% of aggregate SHAP weight, reinforcing that they add nonlinear signal beyond raw inputs.

## Relationship to L1 Subset
- Overlap: `term`, `fico_range_low/high`, `dti`, `inq_last_6mths`, `loan_amnt`, `total_bc_limit`, `num_rev_tl_bal_gt_0`.
- Missing in L1 but important here: `income_to_loan_ratio`, `fico_avg`, `total_rev_hi_lim`, `mort_acc`, `num_actv_rev_tl`, `credit_history_length`.

**What this means:** The sparse logistic model trimmed features that tree models rely on for complex credit-depth interactions. Incorporating these into the neural net should help close the performance gap to H2O.
